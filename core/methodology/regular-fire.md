# Regular FIRE

## Definition

Regular FIRE (sometimes called "Plain FIRE" or "Moderate FIRE") is the standard approach to financial independence, targeting a comfortable but not extravagant retirement lifestyle. It balances savings aggressiveness with reasonable spending, making it the most accessible and sustainable variant for the majority of FIRE aspirants.

## Key Parameters

| Parameter | Typical Value |
|-----------|--------------|
| Annual Expenses | $50,000 – $80,000 |
| FIRE Number (4% rule) | $1,250,000 – $2,000,000 |
| Savings Rate | 40–60% |
| Withdrawal Rate | 3.5–4.0% |
| Target Retirement Age | 40–55 |
| Lifestyle | Comfortable middle-class standard |

## Core Philosophy

Regular FIRE recognizes that **financial independence doesn't require extreme sacrifice**. It targets a sustainable lifestyle where needs are met comfortably, moderate wants are funded, and there's a buffer for unexpected expenses — without requiring geographic arbitrage or radical minimalism.

### The Comfort Zone

Regular FIRE aims for a lifestyle that includes:
- Comfortable housing in a reasonable cost-of-living area
- Regular dining out and entertainment
- Annual vacations (domestic and occasional international)
- Quality healthcare without extreme budgeting
- Hobbies and interests with moderate funding
- Generosity toward family and causes
- A reasonable margin for unexpected expenses

## Calculation Formula

```
Regular FIRE Number = Regular Annual Expenses × 25
```

### Detailed Breakdown

```
Monthly Regular Expenses = Housing + Food + Transport + Healthcare + Utilities + Insurance + Entertainment + Travel + Miscellaneous

Annual Regular Expenses = Monthly Regular Expenses × 12

Regular FIRE Number = Annual Regular Expenses / Withdrawal Rate
```

## Strategies for Regular FIRE

### Balanced Savings Rate
The hallmark of Regular FIRE is a **savings rate that doesn't feel punishing**. A 40–60% savings rate is aggressive by conventional standards but leaves substantial room for living.

### Career Optimization
- Negotiate salary increases and promotions
- Pursue high-demand skills and certifications
- Consider strategic job changes for 10–20% salary bumps
- Leverage remote work for geographic flexibility without relocation

### Investment Allocation
- **Core**: Low-cost total market index funds (VTI, VTSAX, FZROX)
- **International**: International index funds (VXUS, VTIAX)
- **Bonds**: Bond allocation increases with age (10–30%)
- **Alternatives**: REITs, I-Bonds for diversification

### Tax Optimization
- Max out 401(k)/403(b) contributions ($23,500 in 2025)
- Backdoor Roth IRA ($7,000 in 2025)
- HSA triple tax advantage ($4,150 individual / $8,300 family in 2025)
- Tax-loss harvesting in taxable accounts
- Roth conversion ladder for early retirement bridge

## Pros

| Advantage | Explanation |
|-----------|-------------|
| **Sustainable lifestyle** | Not too restrictive, not too lavish — balanced |
| **Broadest appeal** | Suitable for most income levels and life situations |
| **Buffer for surprises** | Higher expenses create margin for unexpected costs |
| **Social compatibility** | Easier to maintain normal social life and relationships |
| **Family-friendly** | Can accommodate children and family needs |
| **Lower burnout risk** | Aggressive but not extreme savings rate |

## Cons

| Disadvantage | Explanation |
|-------------|-------------|
| **Longer timeline** | 12–20 years vs. 8–15 for Lean FIRE |
| **Larger FIRE number** | Requires $1.25M–$2M+ in accumulated wealth |
| **Lifestyle creep risk** | Moderate spending can gradually increase over time |
| **Market sensitivity** | Larger portfolio means larger swings during volatility |
| **Delayed gratification** | Still requires significant sacrifice during accumulation |
| **Complexity** | More budget categories to track and optimize |

## Who Regular FIRE Is For

- **Middle-class families** seeking a comfortable but earlier retirement
- **Dual-income households** where combined savings can reach moderate targets
- **People who enjoy their current lifestyle** and don't want to radically cut spending
- **Those with moderate risk tolerance** who want a buffer against uncertainty
- **Parents** who need to fund children's activities, education, and healthcare
- **People in MCOL areas** where reasonable spending covers a good life
- **Anyone who tried Lean FIRE** and found it too restrictive

## The Path to Regular FIRE

### Phase 1: Accumulation (Years 1–5)
- Maximize savings rate to 40–60%
- Build emergency fund (3–6 months)
- Eliminate high-interest debt
- Maximize tax-advantaged accounts
- Invest aggressively (80–90% equities)

### Phase 2: Growth (Years 5–10)
- Portfolio crosses $500K–$750K
- Compounding accelerates
- Reassess spending targets
- Consider part-time or sabbatical
- Begin tax planning for early retirement

### Phase 3: Transition (Years 10–15+)
- Portfolio approaches FIRE number
- Build cash缓冲 (1–2 years expenses)
- Plan Roth conversion ladder
- Test retirement budget in practice
- Develop post-retirement purpose and activities

## Key Takeaway

Regular FIRE is the **most balanced and sustainable** path to financial independence. It doesn't require extreme frugality or extreme wealth — just disciplined, intentional spending and investing. For most people, this is the variant that offers the best combination of achievable timeline and comfortable lifestyle.
