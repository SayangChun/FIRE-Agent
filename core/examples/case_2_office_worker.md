## Risk Analysis

1. **Negative Net Worth**: Mortgage dominates balance sheet. Illiquid real estate equity.
2. **Single Income Risk**: One job loss event could derail savings.
3. **Mortgage Rate Risk**: If variable rate, rising rates increase expenses.
4. **Lifestyle Risk**: 9,000/month expenses are moderate but could expand.
5. **Career Plateau Risk**: Income may stagnate at 15,000/month without advancement.

### Risk Mitigations
- Prioritize mortgage paydown to free cashflow
- Build 6-month emergency fund (54,000) separate from investments
- Diversify income streams (side project, freelance)
- Consider refinancing to fixed-rate mortgage if variable
- Avoid lifestyle inflation as income grows

---

## Recommended Actions

### Priority 1: Immediate (0-3 months)
1. Build emergency fund to 54,000 (6 months expenses)
2. Review mortgage terms — refinance to fixed if variable rate
3. Set up automatic investment of 6,000/month into diversified ETFs

### Priority 2: Short-term (3-12 months)
4. Create a debt paydown schedule — target mortgage principal reduction
5. Negotiate salary increase or pursue promotion (target 18,000/month)
6. Rebalance investment portfolio to 70/20/10 (stocks/bonds/cash)

### Priority 3: Long-term (1-5 years)
7. Redirect mortgage payment to investments once paid off
8. Explore rental income from real estate if applicable
9. Consider geographic arbitrage or remote work for income boost
10. Annual portfolio rebalancing and FIRE progress review

---

## Key Takeaway

This office worker faces the classic middle-class dilemma: good income but heavy mortgage burden. The fastest path to FIRE is **mortgage acceleration** — once the 800,000 mortgage is eliminated, the freed 4,000/month can nearly double the investment rate. Combined with income growth, age 45-48 FIRE is realistic.
