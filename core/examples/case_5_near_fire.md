## Risk Analysis

1. **Late-Career Risk**: At 42, career recovery from job loss is harder. Income continuity is critical.
2. **Healthcare Gap**: Pre-retirement healthcare (before public insurance eligibility) could be expensive.
3. **Sequence-of-Returns Risk**: 3-year window means a market downturn at 43-44 could delay FIRE.
4. **Inflation Risk**: Retirement expenses may be underestimated if healthcare/elderly costs rise.
5. **Dependency Risk**: Family obligations (children's education, elderly parents) could increase expenses.
6. **Behavioral Risk**: The "one more year" syndrome — difficulty pulling the trigger on retirement.

### Risk Mitigations
- Build 24-month cash buffer (360,000) to ride out market downturns at retirement
- Purchase supplemental health insurance before retirement
- Stress-test portfolio against 2008-style crash (−40%) at age 43-44
- Create a "retirement readiness checklist" with clear go/no-go criteria
- Consider partial retirement / consulting to ease transition

## Recommended Actions

### Priority 1: Immediate (0-3 months)
1. Build cash reserve to 360,000 (2 years expenses) in money market fund
2. Pay off minor loan (50,000) to eliminate obligations
3. Review and optimize investment allocation for 3-year horizon:
   - Increase bond allocation from 16% to 25% for stability
   - Maintain stock allocation at 60% for growth
4. Get comprehensive health insurance review

### Priority 2: Short-term (3-12 months)
5. Create detailed retirement budget — verify 120,000/year is realistic
6. Model healthcare costs for ages 42-60 (pre-Medicare equivalent)
7. Test "retirement drawdown" — live on 120,000/year for 6 months to validate
8. Begin transition planning: what will you do in retirement?

### Priority 3: Long-term (1-5 years)
9. At age 44-45: formal retirement readiness assessment
10. Consider barista FIRE option (part-time consulting 10hrs/week)
11. Set up systematic withdrawal plan
12. Estate planning and legacy considerations

---

## Key Takeaway

This is a **"last mile" FIRE case** — 61.7% of the way there at 42. The math works: with a 40% savings rate and conservative 7% returns, FIRE is achievable by 46. The real challenge is **behavioral, not mathematical**. The key actions are: (1) build a cash buffer to de-risk the sequence-of-returns window, (2) verify the 120,000/year retirement budget is accurate, and (3) prepare psychologically for the identity transition from worker to retiree. The "one more year" trap is the biggest risk at this stage.
